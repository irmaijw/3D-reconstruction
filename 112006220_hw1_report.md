
# 3D Scene Reconstruction

Student 112006220 | Replica apartment_0 | Revised 29 October 2026. This report evaluates an RGB-D reconstruction pipeline on the supplied first-floor dataset and student-collected upstairs and downstairs routes. The main finding is that coverage and registration accuracy must be assessed separately: more observations did not guarantee a coherent map.


## 1. Reconstruction pipeline

Depth unprojection is implemented from scratch in NumPy. For stored integer depth D, metric depth is Z=D/s. The capture intrinsics supply width, height and horizontal FOV; fx=fy=W/(2 tan(hfov/2)), cx=W/2, cy=H/2 under square pixels. Then X=(u-cx)Z/fx and Y=(v-cy)Z/fy. Only finite positive depth is retained. No Open3D projection function is used for this required step.

The supplied capture has no depth_scale field, so integer depths use the collector convention s=1000 units/m; a recorded depth_scale takes precedence. Floating depths are treated as metres. OpenCV BGR is converted to normalized RGB. Camera axes are +X right, +Y down and +Z forward.

Each cloud is voxel-downsampled, assigned estimated normals, and described by FPFH. Mutual feature matching with geometric checks feeds RANSAC for a current-to-previous transform [1]. Open3D point-to-plane ICP refines it [2]. The pose chain is T_global = T_global @ T_current_to_previous. Copied clouds are transformed and merged; map-only downsampling does not alter estimated camera poses.

| Setting | Value |
|---|---|
| Registration / merged-map voxel | 0.05 m / 0.025 m |
| Normals | radius 0.10 m; max 30 neighbors |
| FPFH | radius 0.25 m; max 100 neighbors |
| RANSAC | 3 samples; 100000 trials; confidence 0.999 |
| RANSAC checks | edge ratio 0.9; correspondence gate 0.075 m |
| Point-to-plane ICP | gate 0.075 m; 100 iterations; tolerances 1e-6 |
| Reproducibility | RANSAC seed 0; OMP_NUM_THREADS=1 |


## 2. Evaluation and visualization

Mean L2=(1/N) sum ||p_est,i-p_GT,i||, in metres. Both trajectories use the first consumed camera as reference. GT translation is R0^T(t_i-t0), with axis conversion diag(1,-1,-1) for estimated image-camera positions. Frame identities preserve pose association for subsets. No GT-based fitting, scale adjustment, initialization or registration correction is applied.

Estimated trajectories are red and GT trajectories black. Ceiling crops affect map presentation only. Optional 2 cm TSDF surface fusion uses the saved estimated poses and 8 cm truncation [4]; it does not change trajectory error. A corrected above-floor camera improves visibility but cannot repair distorted geometry.

## 4. Reading evidence cautiously

High fitness can occur on a feature-poor plane and does not prove a correct camera pose. Low inlier RMSE ignores unmatched geometry; zero RMSE with zero correspondences is a failure, not perfect registration. Point yield, pair support, RGB/depth observations, trajectory disagreement and visible map artifacts must be inspected together. Pairwise factors alone do not establish the cause of accumulated drift.

## Temporal multi-start ICP: a controlled improvement

The temporal treatment retains the identical full 388-frame input. Each pair still runs RANSAC, then Open3D point-to-plane ICP from RANSAC, identity and previous estimated-motion initializations. Non-finite, zero-support, >1 m translation and >60 degree rotation candidates are rejected. Among candidates within 0.01 of best fitness, choose minimum RMSE + 0.005 times translation/rotation change from previous motion. GT is not used.

Mean L2 falls from 2.092883 to 0.390671 m, an 81.3% same-frame reduction. On the same upstairs 262-frame subset, temporal poses score 0.209269 m versus standard 0.254445 m. All 3098 factors are recorded; 141/114/132 pairs choose RANSAC/identity/previous-motion starts, with no held-motion fallback. Coarse-stage measurements match the original baseline. This supports this treatment on this capture, not a guarantee for other routes.

The images also differ in representation: the original is merged points; the temporal image uses estimated-pose TSDF. Smoothing and camera changes must not be attributed entirely to more accurate poses. Neither fusion nor visualization changes the measured trajectory.

## Why the map differs from the handout

The handout illustrates a coherent floor layout viewed from above, with estimated and GT paths close together. Our new maps contain displaced copies of surfaces. Sequential reconstruction composes local transforms; a wrong pair alignment can shift or rotate the following observations. Revisiting a room then adds inconsistent geometry instead of automatically correcting earlier poses. The implemented pipeline has no loop-closure pose-graph optimization.

| Upstairs frame range | Mean position error (m) |
|---|---|
| 1-100 | 0.023648 |
| 101-200 | 1.313437 |
| 201-300 | 2.631858 |
| 301-400 | 2.024992 |

The first 100 upstairs frames average about 2.4 cm error, but frames 201-300 average 2.63 m. Failure therefore starts before the final stair section. This GT-based breakdown is post-run evaluation only; it did not guide selection or enter reconstruction. Detailed values are preserved in trajectory_error_blocks.json.

Sampled RGB views include dark and saturated regions under the configured spatial perturbations, and logs contain low-mutual-correspondence warnings. These observations motivate checking local geometry and matching support, but do not prove that exposure caused the depth-registration failure. Missing surfaces may reflect unobserved areas, invalid depth, misalignment or cropping. Their individual contributions were not isolated.

The original Route03 evidence provides a clear caution: frame 293 has zero ICP correspondences and zero inlier RMSE; very sparse views have as few as 162 voxelized points. High median fitness (0.96916) did not prevent its full-route drift. The new factor plots likewise remain diagnostic measurements rather than qualification labels.


# Discussion and Required Questions


## Cross-phase findings

The supplied first-floor baseline is the most accurate measured full capture (0.058835 m). Both original frame-selection interventions worsened matched-frame accuracy. Temporal multi-start ICP improved the original upstairs route on identical frames, but did not prevent drift in the longer captures. More frames increased observed coverage and computation without guaranteeing better geometry. Floors, route scope, perturbations and view sequences differ, so cross-capture scores are descriptive, not controlled ablations.


## Q1. What happens without global registration?

ICP is local. With sufficiently small motion and overlap, identity initialization may work; with larger rotations, gaps or repeated structures, nearest-neighbor matches can converge to an incorrect local solution or lack support. FPFH/RANSAC provides a broader initial alignment [1,2]. No standalone ICP-only ablation was measured. The temporal method still runs RANSAC and compares multiple ICP starts; its identity choices are not an ICP-only experiment.


## Q2. Which choices improve ICP stability?

Implemented choices include 5 cm voxels, normals within 10 cm, a 7.5 cm correspondence gate and bounded point-to-plane ICP. The temporal treatment adds alternative initializations and motion/support checks. Its measured benefit is specific to the original 388-frame capture. A 1 cm Huber pilot did not improve Phase 1 and is not adopted. Multi-scale ICP and loop-closure optimization are future experiments, not completed improvements.


## Q3. How do factors and collection choices relate to results?

Raw/voxelized counts quantify available support; fitness and correspondence counts indicate pairwise matching under a specified gate; inlier RMSE describes accepted residuals. None establishes global correctness alone. Intermediate overlapping views can ease matching, while close-wall, weak-geometry, large-turn and sparse-depth views can be difficult. More frames may add useful rooms or redundant and difficult observations. Matched-frame comparisons distinguish real registration improvements from dropping difficult route sections.


## Limitations and next experiments

Coverage is assessed visually, not with a quantitative map-completeness metric. Routes include stair views and are not strictly upstairs-only. New captures currently have baseline results only. Timings are single runs with shared machine load, not isolated performance benchmarks. The custom ICP bonus is not attempted. No zero-error or reference-quality reconstruction is claimed.

Next steps are to locate weak pairs using recorded factors and input views, test justified selection or registration changes under new declarations, and compare identical frames plus visible coverage. Loop closures could address revisit inconsistency, but require implementation and validation. Current results do not justify replacing the earlier lower-error upstairs run simply because the new capture is longer.
